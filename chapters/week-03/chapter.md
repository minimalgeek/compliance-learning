# Week 3 · Who should approve the distributor payment?

An accounts-payable clerk at DunaWorks has an invoice for EUR 40,000. The description says “market support.” A sales director insists it must be paid before a public employer closes its tender for applicant-ranking software. The intermediary exists in the vendor system. It even has a green review badge. The clerk cannot find a contract or tell what service anyone delivered.

DunaWorks is a fictional Hungarian-headquartered group developing and selling AI recruiting software in Europe and the US. Follow this invoice in two directions. Procurement asks whether the firm can engage the intermediary. An investigator later asks what actually happened. These teams need some of the same records, but their decisions and access rights differ.

> Magyarul: Az átvilágítás során azt vizsgáljuk, kivel és milyen feltételekkel üzletelhetünk. Egy későbbi bejelentés kivizsgálásakor már azt kell feltárni, mi történt egy konkrét ügyben.

**Outcome and schedule.** Build an onboarding decision record and a restricted investigation plan. Spend 50 minutes reading this chapter, 70 on the original sources, two hours on the case and one hour interviewing a practitioner and writing notes. Policies, dates and participants in DunaWorks cases are invented. The SAP reading describes a real enforcement matter.

## Ask what the intermediary actually does

An **intermediary** acts between a company and someone it wants to reach. A reseller might license the recruiting platform to an employer. Ask whether it also changes the model, sells it under its own brand or promises automatic rejection. Product counsel needs those facts to assess AI responsibilities. Finance still needs evidence of the service underlying the commission. A sales agent may introduce customers for a commission. An installer may deal with government offices for permits. The label on the vendor record tells you little about the work, payment flows or people the intermediary can influence.

A **bribe** involves an improper benefit intended to influence a decision. A trip, a job for a relative or an inflated subcontract can raise questions as well as a cash payment. Legal elements differ by jurisdiction. **Gifts and hospitality** include meals, travel or entertainment. Context matters. Ask who receives the benefit, who decides the tender, who pays, why now and what company rules apply. A **conflict of interest** arises when a personal interest could interfere with someone's business duty. A buyer whose sibling owns the agent may need to disclose the relationship and step aside even if every invoice is genuine.

**Competition risk** arises when businesses that should compete coordinate commercially sensitive decisions. If an agent offers a rival's confidential bid sheet at a sales meeting, preserve the facts and involve competition counsel. The relationships, information exchanged and intended use differ from the question about a possible bribe.

**A real comparison.** In January 2024 the US Department of Justice (DOJ) described SAP agents' involvement in bribery schemes connected with government business in South Africa and Indonesia. Its account also describes falsification of records in the South African scheme. Read the transaction descriptions and ask where evidence from a sales, procurement or finance system could have been compared. The DunaWorks invoice is a separate teaching case. Its vague description justifies a question and cannot settle intent. [DOJ case summary](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/sap-pay-over-220m-resolve-foreign-bribery-investigations)

## Three reviews that often share a search box

**Due diligence** means collecting and assessing enough information for a particular decision. Procurement may ask about a vendor's owners, qualifications, business need and reputation. The bank processing its payment may separately perform **customer due diligence (CDD)** under applicable anti-money laundering (AML) rules. A trade specialist may review whether the counterparties, goods, end use or destination are restricted. A passport or registry extract can feed several processes, but each reviewer has a different question.

| Review | Decision and typical owner | What a name search leaves open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supplier or intermediary review | Procurement and compliance assess whether to engage the party, for what services and on which conditions | Who benefits, what work is necessary and whether compensation makes sense |
| Customer AML review | A covered institution's financial-crime team identifies its customer and beneficial owners, understands the relationship and monitors it according to risk | Who ultimately owns or controls the customer and whether activity fits its profile |
| Sanctions review | A sanctions specialist assesses restrictions under the applicable regime for parties and transactions | Identity, ownership, relevant jurisdiction, restrictions and any applicable authorization |
| Export review | A trade specialist assesses goods, software or technology, destination, recipient and intended use | Classification, licensing and end-use questions even for an identified customer |

A **beneficial owner** is the natural person who ultimately owns or controls the entity under the relevant rules. A director signing a form may represent the entity without owning it. A possible sanctions list match is a lead. Ask for disambiguating identifiers, the list and version, ownership facts and a specialist's disposition. The US Treasury's OFAC framework concerns US sanctions. A Hungarian entity needs its own jurisdictional assessment.

The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) banking guidance explains a **risk-based approach**: direct more attention to higher exposure. In the bank example, unusual account activity goes to financial-crime operations. A specialist evaluates suspicion and any reporting obligation under local law. The software provider's onboarding analyst cannot decide the bank's reporting duty.

## Design the distributor review around a real decision

DunaWorks policy permits only an authorized procurement manager to activate a distributor after compliance review. Finance separately authorizes payments. Sales owns the business rationale. No one can self-approve an exception. These invented decision rights make it possible to test what a green badge actually authorizes.

| Stage | Work and evidence | Decision or handoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intake | Sales names the legal entity, territory, public customer, proposed service, fee and payment account | Procurement checks identity and asks why an intermediary is needed. |
| Risk classification | Compliance records public-official contact, success fee, ownership uncertainty and information sources | Enhanced review gets a named reviewer and unresolved questions. |
| Due diligence | Verify ownership, experience, references, associated parties and conflicts. Compare the proposed fee with the work. | Record each source, date and conflicting fact. Request evidence for gaps. |
| Terms and approval | Define deliverables, permitted activities, fee, review rights and restrictions on subcontractors | Approver can decline, hold or approve within specific conditions and dates. |
| First payment | Match invoice to contract, completed deliverable, approved payee and bank account | Finance releases or holds this payment. Onboarding approval alone cannot release it. |
| Ongoing review | Watch changes to ownership, bank account, territory, commission or subcontractors | Owner reopens review on change and at the risk-based review date. |

Suppose the approved scope was “recruitment software integration in Germany.” Sales now wants the same partner to introduce it to a public buyer in another territory. The badge can be accurate about the old integration contract and misleading about the new assignment. Store the relationship, role, scope, approval date and terms on the record. A one-bit vendor flag loses exactly the facts a payment reviewer needs.

## A report arrives while the payment is on hold

A warehouse employee reports that the sales director told the intermediary it would receive a fee for “getting us past the committee.” The report contains a date and a screenshot. **Triage** is the initial decision about urgency, preservation, conflicts and who should handle the matter. Assign a case identifier and restrict access. An intake coordinator acknowledges the report through the safe channel, explains follow-up and checks whether the named director controls that channel.

**Confidentiality** limits who can see information. **Anonymity** means the reporter's identity is unknown to the recipient. A named reporter can request confidentiality. An anonymous report may contain details that indirectly identify its author. Avoid promising absolute secrecy. Give the payment team the operational instruction it needs while keeping the allegation and reporter details in the restricted case. **Non-retaliation** means protecting people from reprisals for raising concerns. Assign someone independent to follow up if, for example, a reporter loses shifts shortly after speaking up.

An investigator writes the question as a testable allegation: “On or around 8 October, did the sales director authorize an improper payment connected to this tender?” Record who can investigate impartially. Collect the original communication with context, agreement versions, procurement file, bank instructions and tender timeline through authorized channels. Keep an evidence log with source, date, custodian and access. Interview relevant people separately and document facts both supporting and weakening the allegation.

The investigator distinguishes established facts, disputed accounts and gaps. The authorized decision-maker determines the outcome under the company's procedure and applicable law. “Insufficient evidence” preserves uncertainty. A **root cause** is the underlying condition that allowed a failure. If the payment workflow never checked a change in scope, addressing one employee's conduct leaves that defect in place. Assign a correction, retest another changed-scope invoice and review whether the reporter experienced reprisals.

## Workshop: build two records from one packet

The following packet is synthetic. All dates refer to October. Spend 40 minutes mapping onboarding, 35 drafting the payment decision, 30 on the investigation and 15 challenging your conclusions.

| Item | What it actually says |
| --- | --- |
| R1 / vendor master | Danube Channel Kft. cleared in May for German software integrations. Owner field lists a holding company. |
| R2 / new request | Sales seeks public tender introductions. Fee EUR 40,000. First-page statement of work is unsigned. |
| R3 / deliverable | Calendar invite shows an introductory call was planned. No attendance record or work report. |
| R4 / payment | Invoice names the approved company. New instruction directs payment to a different account holder. |
| R5 / screening | Automated tool returned a possible name match. No date of birth, registration ID or analyst disposition. |
| R6 / confidential intake | Named reporter supplies a screenshot and asks that the sales director not see their name. Original thread pending. |

**Record A, procurement and finance.** Fill in entity, proposed role, trigger, current authority, open checks, owners, interim decision, evidence needed to change it and next review. For each R-item ask what it establishes and what remains unknown. Specify which operational fields finance may see.

**Record B, restricted case.** Fill in allegation, immediate safeguards, conflicts, investigator, sources to preserve, competing explanations, decision authority, follow-up and verified closure. Identify one question that belongs to counsel. Keep reporter details out of routine procurement records.

**Challenge yourself.** Tomorrow a supplier representative sends the missing signed agreement. Which checks does it resolve? If the reported statement turns out to be accurately quoted but concerned an ordinary scheduling fee, what happens to the payment hold? What if the possible sanctions match is cleared first?

## Worked decisions and interview

**Payment record.** Hold invoice R4. The earlier approval covers different work. The holding company does not identify the natural persons who ultimately control the intermediary. A scheduled call does not establish delivery. Sales must document the new role and work, procurement must verify the counterparty and bank details through a trusted channel, and the specialist must resolve the screening hit. Finance sees the hold, its owner, operational reasons and authorized release conditions. A signed agreement addresses the missing execution of that agreement. The mismatch in payee and unanswered service questions survive.

**Case record.** Assign an investigator outside the director's reporting line. Preserve the source thread, contract history and payment records. Ask who participated, what the phrase meant, what the sales director was authorized to promise and whether anyone actually paid. A competing explanation is a legitimate introduction described carelessly. Another is a payment for improper influence. The screenshot alone cannot distinguish them. Counsel assesses any reporting or preservation obligations. Keep the original reported concern and the investigator’s dated determination as separate records. Record the rationale and restrict access to both. [Week 7](../week-07/chapter.md#after-launch-complaints-incidents-and-recovery) applies the same distinction to a customer complaint.

**Two separate closures.** Clearing an erroneous name match resolves only the sanctions question. A credible innocent explanation for the remark may resolve the allegation while the invoice still lacks evidence of delivery. Conversely, cancelling the invoice does not resolve whether someone tried to misuse the process. A reviewer can close the process weakness after procurement demonstrates a changed-scope request triggers fresh approval.

**Interview.** Ask a procurement analyst to walk through the last distributor file returned to sales. Which fact changed the decision? How long did the file wait? Ask the reviewer what information could have been shared with finance. A product hypothesis is a pre-payment comparison of contract scope, deliverable and payee. The finance analyst uses it, procurement resolves changes, the compliance specialist reviews escalations, and the controller is a possible buyer. Pilot access would require approved invoice and contract extracts. Measure accepted follow-ups, reviewer time and missed scope changes. Keep the confidential case outside the pilot.
