# Week 5 · Which business activity does this rule change?

DunaWorks is changing its AI recruitment product. Yesterday it organized CVs by date. The proposed release scores applicants and recommends whom to interview. The model supplier calls the update administrative. Which facts would let the provider’s compliance officer assess that claim?

An **applicability assessment** connects a particular provision to a legal entity, role, activity, territory and date. In this chapter you will reconstruct a change using a real published EU regulation and fictional business records. Then you will apply the same method to five proposed uses of artificial intelligence (AI).

## Separate the source from the interpretation

A **proposal** signals a possible change. An **adopted legal act** has an authoritative text, publication history and provisions governing timing. **Guidance** explains an authority's view. A **contract** can commit a named party to something more specific. A company's interpretation explains why it thinks an obligation applies. Record the source and the interpretation separately so one can be revised without rewriting the other.

| Item reaching the inbox | What to record | What a reviewer should do next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Newsletter says a change is coming | Publisher, date, link to the underlying act, claimed impact | Locate the official source and determine its status. |
| Official Journal publishes an EU regulation | Identifier, articles, publication, dates, transitional rules, amendments | Extract the operative provision and record the business facts needed for scope. |
| An authority publishes a guide | Issuer, date, audience and cited provision | Use it to understand interpretation and identify unresolved local questions. |
| A customer sends a contract addendum | Parties, services, proposed or signed version, effective date | Compare promised behavior with the legal baseline and current capability. |
| Counsel signs a scope memo | Author, date, facts assumed, conclusion and next review | Link affected controls; reopen if a material fact changes. |

**Entry into force** and **date of application** can differ. Individual provisions may also have different dates. An amendment introduces another version. A useful obligation register preserves the text that informed a decision at the time, as well as the current assessment. Mark local implementation questions explicitly when working with a directive.

**A change inside the company can trigger review.** A document editor now recommends which applicants to interview. Compare the approved purpose, new specification, sales claims and recruiter workflow. The same law and same customer can yield a different scope assessment after a product change.

## Six specialties through one factory

For the industrial specialties, consider a separate German manufacturer that installs a new line, hires agency workers and starts shipping a maintenance kit. Before making a country-by-country legal checklist, learn which business facts the specialists need. The examples below are routing questions. They do not establish that an individual statute applies.

| Specialty | Trigger and decisive facts | Person and evidence to seek |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employment and safety | Agency workers operate new equipment. Who directs the work, where, and what hazards changed? | Site safety lead and HR inspect task assessment, training and incident records. |
| Environment | A coating process changes. What substance, quantity, emission, waste stream and site permit are involved? | Environmental manager compares process specifications with permit conditions. |
| Supply-chain due diligence | A new component comes from a different supplier and site. Which products, entities and upstream impacts are involved? | Procurement and sustainability trace source, grievance channels and corrective actions. |
| Product regulation | Consumer maintenance kit offered online in Germany. What product, economic role and sales channel are involved? | Product safety and local commercial counsel inspect listing, labeling and technical file. |
| Tax | One entity invoices while another dispatches cross-border goods. Who sells, where are goods and customer, and what documents exist? | Tax specialist traces invoice, order and transport records. |
| AI governance | Recruitment team buys software to rank candidates. Whose prospects change and how is the ranking used? | HR, privacy and legal inspect vendor documentation, actual configuration and hiring workflow. |

A supplier declaration may help establish where a part was made. It says little about how the German site trained a worker or which entity owes tax on a sale. Preserve the link between a specific question and the evidence capable of answering it. If a supplier cannot trace the upstream facility, record the gap with a named owner; a guessed country turns uncertainty into incorrect data.

## Worked law: assessing a candidate-ranking change

The Hungarian DunaWorks entity markets the system under its own name. A German employer configures the vacancy and decides whom to interview. An upstream supplier hosts the model. Record each party’s actions, the affected people and the market. A model vendor’s general description cannot establish the intended purpose of DunaWorks’ finished system.

Annex III point 4(a) covers specified employment uses including evaluating candidates. Counsel checks the classification rules in Article 6 against the actual ranking function, records the applicable dates and examines the human-oversight requirements where applicable. Product supplies the release description and recruiter walkthrough. The analyst records which facts could change counsel’s assessment. ## Workshop: write the impact record and verify the change

**Case packet.** The approved specification describes a recruiter choosing candidates after reviewing each application. Revision R12 adds suggested rejections. A sales deck calls them automatic. The designer shows an override button. QA demonstrates that an administrator can reverse a suggestion. Nobody has demonstrated this with an ordinary recruiter account. DunaWorks’ fictional pilot criterion requires an ordinary recruiter to be able to disregard a suggestion before it affects the hiring queue.

Spend 70 minutes on the impact record and 50 on the inventory below. Identify the disputed fact, accountable owner, evidence needed, interim recommendation and reopening trigger. Decide what an agent could extract from the specification, sales deck and QA run.

| Worked field | DunaWorks assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Source and interpretation | Record the version of the Act, relevant provisions, counsel’s dated rationale and applicability dates. Link the internal pilot criterion separately. |
| Potential impact | A rejection could take effect before anyone assesses it. Confirm the default, permissions, timing and applicant experience. |
| Evidence gap | The administrator run establishes administrator access. Recruiter access and the timing of the queue change remain unknown. |
| Action and decision | QA tests R12 with a recruiter account and traces one fictional applicant through rejection and override. Product reconciles the sales claim with actual behavior. Compliance recommends holding the pilot pending review. |
| Verification | Capture build, tenant settings, actor, timestamps and final queue state. A reviewer checks the result against the pilot criterion. Reopen if the default, model, permissions or intended use changes. |

**Platform opportunity.** An agent can flag the conflicting claims, cite both versions and request role-matched evidence. Counsel owns the legal interpretation. A successful override test supports one product claim. The broader review still needs evidence that recruiters understand the output and have time and authority to intervene.

## An AI inventory that survives a product change

An **AI inventory** records actual uses, their owners and the decisions they influence. One vendor can supply several systems, and the same model can draft an email or rank job applicants. An EU AI Act **provider** generally develops or has a system developed and places it on the market or puts it into service under its name or trademark. A **deployer** uses a system under its authority in the relevant sense. Multiple parties and roles may be involved. Capture the facts before assigning duties. Check scope, definitions, intended purpose, classification provisions and applicable dates. See the Commission readings.

| Use, owner and purpose | People, data and supplier | Role question and reviewer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HR ranks applications to choose interviewees | Candidates; CVs and scores; Recruit-A vendor | Internal deployer candidate. Employment selection is listed in Annex III. HR and legal examine intended use, Article 6, actual reliance and applicable timing. |
| Support drafts incident replies for agent editing | Customer contacts; tickets; HídDesk model | Is the feature used under DunaWorks' authority? Privacy and support check reuse, instructions, audience and human review. |
| Engineering detects unexpected changes in ranking scores | Applicants and recruiters indirectly affected by release decisions, evaluation runs, in-house detector | Who acts on the alert? QA checks detector coverage, escalation and the release owner’s authority. |
| Sales translates standard brochures | Prospective readers; marketing text; Lingua-B vendor | Who deploys the tool and publishes the translation? Marketing checks whether errors change safety or contractual claims. |
| Software team sells a branded candidate-ranking feature | Clients and applicants; hiring records; third-party model inside DunaWorks software | Could DunaWorks be provider of the finished system? Product counsel examines branding, contracts, intended purpose and supply chain. |

**Exercise and worked answer.** A salesperson says the first and last rows have the same classification because they use the same model. List three facts that might change who must act. DunaWorks' relationship to the market differs, its intended purpose and documentation matter, and internal use differs from supplying a branded system to clients. Review the relevant high-risk provisions and the current implementation timeline. A human pressing “confirm” does not by itself resolve how a ranking affects applicants. Preserve reviewer, assessment date, outstanding facts and re-review trigger.

## Run the register after the first assessment

Imagine legal discovers that the upstream model supplier changed its scoring method. The API and version label might still look identical. A useful system can reopen a question without announcing a breach. The analyst compares the updated facts to the prior decision, asks which assumption changed, and records either a reason for retaining the assessment or actions with owners.

A practical register has four linked records. The **source record** stores issuer, jurisdiction, text, provision, version and dates. The **interpretation** names the reviewer, business facts and applicability rationale. The **implementation task** names the control owner, required behavior, due date and evidence. The **verification record** describes the test, result and remaining limits. One source can lead to several local interpretations. One software release can close some tasks while another market remains open.

**Operating rhythm.** Compliance monitors relevant official feeds and business-change notices. A weekly triage allocates owners. The specialist decides scope or asks for missing facts. Business owners implement changes. Reviewers retest the affected operation. A monthly management report identifies decisions awaiting expertise, overdue actions and tested closures. Use different dates for publication, legal application, internal target and actual verification.

**Practitioner interview.** Ask a compliance lead to show the last change they marked “no impact.” What source and business facts supported that conclusion? Who could challenge it? Then ask for one implementation ticket that reopened. What changed? A founder might test a service that flags a stale assessment when a product document changes. Inputs are approved changes, business inventory and reviewed interpretations. A compliance analyst triages; counsel approves new interpretations; a product owner acts. The possible buyer is the head of compliance operations. Measure analyst time, missed affected activities and false escalations against practitioner-reviewed examples.
