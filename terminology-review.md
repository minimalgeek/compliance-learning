# Course terminology review

Review baseline: commit `ccb7d91`. Scope includes the shared EN–HU taxonomy, the seven chapter glossaries and their worked examples, the learning plan and course guidance. This is a decision list. The proposed wording below awaits review, starting with item 1. Legal-role entries concern terminology and do not reassess any particular product’s legal classification.

| ID | Where and what the text says | Proposed resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | [Taxonomy](taxonomy.md) introduces “Risk domain / sub-risk” through an applicant losing an interview. [Week 7](chapters/week-07/chapter.md#the-vocabulary-of-rules-and-exposure) defines a risk scenario by how someone might suffer harm. The compliance-risk exercise starts with a party potentially missing an obligation. A learner could enter three different kinds of information in one field. | Define domain as a grouping, sub-risk as a reusable risk type, and scenario as the specific way an obligation could be missed. Keep the potential impact on people visible. Mark wider AI risks explicitly when the example teaches those. |
| 2 | [Taxonomy](taxonomy.md) combines source and obligation. The learning plan’s obligation row lists metadata but omits the duty bearer and required conduct. | Separate the cited passage from the extracted duty. Record who owes what, to whom where relevant, under which conditions and by when. Identify contract, internal policy and law separately. |
| 3 | [Week 6](chapters/week-06/chapter.md#test-the-agents-links) gives O1 as a requirement for a recruiter to review an application. R1 and R2 are software workflow failures. The fixture leaves open whether the employer owes the review or DunaWorks owes the capability to enforce it. | State the provider’s obligation and the employer’s operating responsibility separately. Test each claim against evidence from the party able to perform it. |
| 4 | [Learning plan](learning-plan.md#reference-for-all-seven-weeks) lists recurring objects without CMT. The taxonomy provides a CMT row but its opening could suggest that every listed term is specific to FordoX. | Identify CMT as FordoX’s name for an operational compliance topic. Include it in the plan and distinguish topic, binding duty and risk category. |
| 5 | [Week 7](chapters/week-07/chapter.md#the-vocabulary-of-rules-and-exposure) illustrates tolerance using full eligible-ID coverage. Its answer key later explains that this pass criterion cannot establish a residual rating. | Use three labels: test acceptance criterion, approved risk tolerance, assessed residual risk. Identify whose approval defines the tolerance. |
| 6 | [Week 7](chapters/week-07/chapter.md#the-vocabulary-of-controls-and-cases) calls “recruiter can reach every eligible ID” the control and a release test its execution. Elsewhere reconciliation is itself a detective control. | Name the control objective, implemented safeguard, execution and test. A reconciliation can be a control in its own right. Say which control each log or run belongs to. |
| 7 | [Week 2](chapters/week-02/chapter.md#classify-the-evidence) distinguishes assessment and control evidence well. [Taxonomy](taxonomy.md) lists one verification status alongside expected evidence and verified effectiveness. A verified source or one successful run could be mistaken for verification of the whole control. | Attach evidence role to the claim. Distinguish a requested artifact, a received artifact, the reviewer’s check and the conclusion about effectiveness for a scope and period. |
| 8 | [Taxonomy](taxonomy.md#from-assessment-to-decision) places Remediate, Escalate and Block in a single list. [Week 7](chapters/week-07/chapter.md#answer-key-and-a-reusable-decision-record) recommends remediation while keeping the pilot on hold. A single status field loses part of the decision. | Teach risk disposition, referral to a higher authority, finding status and deployment decision as related records. Define how a temporary hold differs from a formal block. |
| 9 | [Week 7 glossary](chapters/week-07/chapter.md#englishhungarian-desk-glossary) identifies a risk owner by who can change exposure. An engineer may change exposure without the authority to accept it. The reusable record omits named residual-rating and tolerance fields used in its own answer key. | Specify accountable owner, delegated acceptance authority, control operator, verifier and release owner. Record residual rating or uncertainty, tolerance source, decision expiry and reassessment trigger. |
| 10 | [Week 7 case transitions](chapters/week-07/chapter.md#from-a-signal-to-an-owned-case) jumps from awaiting evidence to assessed, and awaiting retest to verified. One glossary row combines incident and complaint. [Week 3](chapters/week-03/chapter.md#worked-decisions-and-interview) correctly allows one concern to close while another stays open. | Define the missing transitions, including failed retest. Label a complaint as an incoming report and incident assessment as a separate determination. |
| 11 | [Week 5 glossary](chapters/week-05/chapter.md#vocabulary-and-original-readings) describes a provider as making a system available under its name. The chapter body also considers development and placing on the market or putting into service. [Week 4 glossary](chapters/week-04/chapter.md#vocabulary-and-original-readings) reduces processor to “acts on instructions.” | Tighten the short definitions so distributors and ordinary instructed employees are not accidentally classified as providers or processors. Keep the Hungarian legal labels. See AI Act Article 3 and GDPR Article 4. |
| 12 | [Week 4](chapters/week-04/chapter.md#vocabulary-and-original-readings) uses “impact assessment” in DPIA. [Week 5](chapters/week-05/chapter.md#vocabulary-and-original-readings) uses the same words for working out who must implement a regulatory change. The taxonomy also discusses impact on an applicant. | Qualify each use: data protection impact assessment, regulatory change impact assessment, or impact in a risk scenario. Spell out the relevant security consequences in the Week 4 personal data breach entry. |
| 13 | [Week 1 glossary](chapters/week-01/chapter.md#glossary-a) describes attestation as an assertion. [Week 2](chapters/week-02/chapter.md#what-an-assurance-report-buys-the-reader) introduces formal assurance work. The learning plan and Week 1 also alternate between assurance as the assessment and the confidence it supports. | Distinguish a management assertion, an individual sign-off, an independent assurance engagement and its conclusion. Translate attestation according to the context. |
| 14 | [Week 6 glossary](chapters/week-06/chapter.md#vocabulary-and-product-readings) renders abstention as *döntéstől való tartózkodás*. The agent is actually withholding a conclusion on incomplete evidence and routing a follow-up. “Ground truth” can also sound stronger than a disputed reviewer label. | Define an agent’s abstention and an authorized person’s decision separately. Name the agreed reference answer, reviewer and unresolved disagreements in evaluations. |
| 15 | [Week 1](chapters/week-01/chapter.md#glossary-a), [Week 2](chapters/week-02/chapter.md#vocabulary-and-original-readings) and [Week 7](chapters/week-07/chapter.md#englishhungarian-desk-glossary) vary between *maradványkockázat* and *fennmaradó kockázat*, *alkalmazhatóság* and *alkalmazandóság*, and several translations of accountability. Week 7 uses *kockázatvállalás* for formal risk acceptance. | Establish preferred EN–HU headwords, retain useful synonyms, and use *a maradványkockázat elfogadása* for the formal acceptance decision. |
| 16 | [Week 1](chapters/week-01/chapter.md#story) uses QA before explaining the role and later uses “model digest” without explaining what is compared. | Expand QA at first use in each self-contained chapter. Explain the digest as an identifier derived from model contents, and distinguish the model version from the application build. |

Terminology sources for item 11 and the breach definition in item 12: [AI Act, Hungarian text, Article 3](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/2026-07-27/hun) and [GDPR, Article 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng).
