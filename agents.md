# Instructions for this compliance learning project

## Purpose and learner

Create learning material for an experienced software developer and CTO exploring a startup that automates corporate compliance.

Use the Pareto principle to build broad, practical literacy: understand the main specialties, recognize recurring workflows, interview practitioners, and identify a bounded product opportunity. Deep specialist study comes after a target customer, activity, and jurisdiction are chosen.

The baseline is the six-week, approximately 30-hour plan in [learning-plan.md](learning-plan.md). Week 1 is the established example of the intended chapter style.

## Read before changing material

Read [readme.md](readme.md), the relevant part of [learning-plan.md](learning-plan.md), and the existing chapter you will extend or change. Use the repository's current contents as the source of truth.

The full project instructions live in this lowercase file, `agents.md`. The uppercase `AGENTS.md` is a discovery entry point and should continue to point here. Keep substantive instructions in one place.

## Explain the jargon

Write the main teaching text in clear English. The learner specifically wants jargon explained with examples and, where useful, Hungarian equivalents or explanations.

- Define an unfamiliar term before relying on it.
- Explain it using ordinary language and a concrete business example.
- Add natural Hungarian help for key terms. Examples: governance = vállalatirányítás; compliance = megfelelőség; assurance = bizonyosságot nyújtó értékelés.
- Explain abbreviations on first use, including the person's actual role behind a job title.
- Use Hungarian translations as learning aids. Identify contextual or approximate translations rather than claiming an official legal translation.
- Avoid circular definitions, unexplained corporate language, and the assumption that a technical background includes legal or audit expertise.

Use examples to distinguish concepts such as governance and management, risk and compliance, policy and control, evidence and conclusion, monitoring and assurance, and responsibility and accountability.

## Preserve the broad scope and Pareto focus

Corporate compliance spans more than cybersecurity, SOC reports, or privacy. Keep the broader field visible: conduct, bribery, competition, trade, financial crime, financial reporting, privacy, technology, resilience, employment and safety, environment, products, supply chains, and AI as appropriate to the requested week.

Prioritize reusable ideas:

1. Understand the business and the requirement's applicability.
2. Identify the objective and risk.
3. Assign ownership and decision authority.
4. Define and operate controls.
5. Gather relevant evidence.
6. Assess results and preserve uncertainty.
7. Investigate concerns, remediate, verify, and report.

Keep depth proportionate to the week's learning objectives. Make specialist detail optional when it does not help the learner understand the workflow or evaluate a startup hypothesis. Avoid implying that a short course confers complete legal coverage.

## Build a chapter, not just a reading list

A requested chapter must teach the material directly and be understandable on its own. Fetching documents and linking to them is supporting work.

Include, as relevant:

- The purpose, learning outcomes, and a realistic reading or study schedule.
- Connected explanations with practical examples.
- A consistent worked company or workflow.
- A glossary with English definitions and Hungarian support.
- Tables or precise diagrams when they clarify roles, mappings, or relationships.
- A practical exercise or reusable working template.
- Worked examples and explained answers for the exercises.
- Source notes, direct original links, editions or dates, and useful reading locations.
- Explicit connections to the founder's discovery and automation goals.

Keep examples consistent. Week 1 uses fictional DunaWorks Group: 2,000 employees, Hungarian headquarters, German and US operations, equipment and subscription-software sales, intermediaries, cloud suppliers, and public- and private-sector customers.

Identify fictional internal policies, thresholds, deadlines, assignments, and data as teaching examples. Their appearance in a worked case does not make them general legal requirements.

## Research and source handling

Fetch and read the suggested authoritative documents before drawing on them. Prefer official regulators, government bodies, standard setters, and the relevant professional organizations. Search snippets alone are insufficient for developing a chapter.

- Record the source title, issuing body, edition or publication date, and a direct URL.
- Use clear source references in the chapter. Cite printed page numbers accurately and distinguish them from PDF viewer indexes when necessary.
- Verify current law, implementation status, and applicability when those facts matter. Distinguish proposals, adopted requirements, guidance, standards, contracts, and internal policies.
- Explain a source's jurisdiction and purpose instead of presenting one regime's guidance as universal.
- Use public overviews and clearly describe that limitation when a full standard is paid or unavailable. Do not claim to have reviewed inaccessible material.
- Write original explanations and examples. Keep quotation and reuse within applicable rights and limits; link to third-party works.
- Preserve material source information when updating content. Date-sensitive statements need fresh checking before a substantive update.

Do not treat a certificate, uploaded document, generated report, or missing record as broader proof than it supports. Distinguish a demonstrated failure from insufficient evidence.

## Connect learning to the startup goal

Frame potential automation opportunities as hypotheses until supported by customer evidence.

For each proposed workflow, identify the trigger, user, inputs, systems, judgments, decision authority, output, reviewer, buyer, and measurable result. Examine whether data access and integrations make a practical pilot possible.

Include practitioner interview prompts that ask for a recent real example. Look for repeated effort, waiting, missing information, rejected evidence, unclear ownership, and weak remediation closure. Keep legal interpretation and consequential judgments tied to appropriate expertise and authority.

Do not equate automation with a guarantee of compliance. Make a proposed product's claim and scope assessable.

## Files and presentation

Use lowercase `readme.md` and `agents.md` as requested. Preserve the uppercase instruction entry point for automatic discovery.

Keep the plan at `learning-plan.md`. Store chapters under `chapters/week-NN/`, with `chapter.md` as the editable reading text and a descriptively named companion PDF. Continue the existing naming convention unless there is a concrete reason to change it.

PDF is the preferred finished reading format established in this project. Maintain useful navigation and clear typography. Keep the Markdown and PDF editions semantically aligned; layout and printed page references may differ.

When creating or updating a PDF:

- Use the applicable document or PDF creation workflow.
- Embed fonts that support Hungarian characters, especially ő and ű.
- Render and inspect every page for clipping, overlap, broken tables, unreadable text, and missing glyphs.
- Check source links, internal navigation, bookmarks, page counts, and page references.
- Ensure examples, questions, and answer keys remain consistent.
- Mark a chapter ready only after its output has been checked.

Update the README's links and material-status table when files are added or changed. Distinguish material readiness from the learner's progress. Preserve existing chapters and create only the requested next material.

## Uncertainty and collaboration

The learner asked to be consulted when something is uncertain. First use the existing discussion, repository, and authoritative sources to resolve the question.

Ask before making a materially uncertain choice that changes the learning goal, content scope, intended meaning, or language approach. State unresolved factual or legal uncertainty clearly. Routine formatting decisions that follow the established approach can proceed without another confirmation.

Finish with a concise description of what changed and the relevant file or repository links. Do not claim an upload, publication, completed chapter, source review, or verification that did not occur.
